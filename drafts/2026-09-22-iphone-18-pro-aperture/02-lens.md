A lens that opens and closes.

Dark video gets more light, Night mode finishes faster, and a group stays sharper when the lens closes down.

The main lens has four openings. f/1.48, f/1.8, f/2.8, and f/4.0. Six blades. A 17 Pro main lens is fixed at f/1.78.

f/1.48 is the wide stop. Apple puts faster Night mode photos, and the line "around 50 percent better low-light," on that opening. The same opening is what Apple means by improved low-light video. The lens opens, and the frame rate can drop. Apple calls the sensor new and does not publish a size. That low-light line is the wider hole.

f/4.0 is the closed stop. More of a group or a room stays sharp. At f/1.48 the background softens a little, up close. It is a phone sensor.

⚠️ Main camera only. Ultra Wide stays 13 mm at f/2.2. Telephoto stays 100 mm at 4x, f/2.8.

Default already moves the blades. When a picture needs a specific opening, open Camera, swipe up from the modes, and select Lens Aperture. Apple's service steps name the lowest circle, f1.48, and the highest, f4.0.

Shutter speed, white balance, and a histogram sit in that same menu. VERIFY the histogram label. Use it when a face is blowing out.

Normal video from the main camera can take a Cinematic blur afterward, up to 4K Dolby Vision at 60 fps. The 17 Pro tops out at 30 fps. VERIFY the taps. Apple also lists smart focus tracking, which holds a subject even after they leave the frame.

Reference Image is an opt-in on the main camera. Settings, then Camera, then Reference Image. Capture is unavailable in the EU at launch. China mainland cannot capture or develop one.
