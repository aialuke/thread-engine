## 2. Contradicts our plan

- **Nothing found that says the planned tests are wrong-headed in direction.** If anything, TypeSafe's own docs and cookbooks converge on the same five questions independently: adversarial/misleading framing is a documented failure mode (#2), option order is called out as significant in a cookbook (#7), a bare abstain option with no confidence gating is exactly the naive design TypeSafe's own consistency cookbooks improve on (#11, #12), and structured option descriptions are recommended without being quantified (#5, #6) — which is what Q5 sets out to quantify.
- **One caution on method, not direction:** confidence-routing docs (#8, #9) suggest the six-vote observation "confidence sits 0.05–0.14 below top probability" might be partly a *mechanical* artifact of how confidence is computed from the full distribution, not a discovery about Jev's honesty. The experiment plan should first check what the documented confidence formula predicts for the card's typical probability spread before treating the gap itself as the finding — otherwise we risk "discovering" arithmetic.
- **Circularity risk flagged by our own prior experiment (#18)** applies with equal force to the design-card work: if the same operator who writes a card's `evidence`/`open_risk` text later judges whether Jev's vote "tracked" that text, the judgment of steering is not independent of the card. B4's fix (reserve some evaluation to a channel the card-writer doesn't touch) is a pattern worth reusing for Q1.

## 4. Hand-offs

- **To the LLM choice-bias reader:** `research/jev-articles/Jev as a judge.md` is a full CMU paper ("JEV-as-a-Judge: Accept When Confident, Escalate When Unsure," Li, Miao, Krishnan, Padman) that independently measures option-order (candidate-position) reversal bias for Jev specifically: "Reversal changes 3.25% of RewardBench decisions and 11.14% of JudgeBench decisions... JEV picks the first position 48.9% and 48.4% of the time, so the inconsistency is not a positional preference." It also measures paraphrase/formulation sensitivity ("four [changed] across 48 paraphrases") and repeated-call determinism ("JEV changes no decision across 96 repeated comparisons on 48 examples"), and documents a style-adversarial gap (elaborate wrong answers pull Jev down 9–20 points versus GPT-6). This is squarely their lens (independent academic bias study of an LLM-adjacent judge) but bears directly on our Q2 and Q4 — worth reconciling with the option-order material here.
- **To the abstention/"none of the above" reader:** the operator's Q3 (conflation of "none fit" with "too close to call" in `ask_luke`) has no TypeSafe-native literature answer beyond generic "add other" guidance (#4) and the confidence-gated coarse/fine pattern (#10). This is exactly the shape of question the abstention-papers lens is built for; the two consistency cookbooks (#11, #12) are the closest TypeSafe gets, and both solve it with a *probability band*, not a second question — worth checking against what the academic abstention literature recommends.
- **To the human survey/decision methods reader:** this repo's `research/jev-test/README.md` Experiment 3 design ("One instrument... every model rater answers the same questions... the reference is the average of the strongest models," and the operator's labels being "one rater among several, with no answer key") is a self-consciously survey-methodological design (blinded raters, disputed-vs-random sampling, `labelling.py sample`) that may be worth their independent read for whether it matches best practice in human-rater study design, separate from anything Jev-specific.

## 5. What earlier repo experiments already settled (don't re-test)

- **Q4, repeat-call determinism for concrete yes/no-shaped questions:** already answered — 0 of 30 repeated verdicts changed in Experiment 3 (finding #15). Don't re-run a plain repeat test on stable, concrete questions; the open surface is wording sensitivity of *graded* questions (Score-type "useful" levels), which flips 5/10 verdicts on Worth-joining under rewording (#15) — treat that as the live risk, not raw non-determinism.
- **Q4, does higher confidence track higher agreement on our rubric:** already answered directionally yes (AUROC 0.75–0.83, finding #16). Don't re-run this as a fresh discovery question; only re-check it per new pull, since B3 already showed the *cut-off* itself doesn't transfer from validation to final (finding #17 — 96.1% → 92.6%).
- **The old "`act` pass rule" and "relevance rule" hypotheses from Experiment 1 are formally withdrawn** (`research/Jev cookbooks.md`, "Withdrawn (28 Sep)" and "Withdrawn hypothesis (27 Sep)") — don't resurrect either as a baseline; the harness copies `act` from Codex and never scores it independently, and the relevance-drop rule would wrongly drop 71 of 126 settled "act" posts.
- **Circularity of Arms 3 and 5 in Experiment 4 is already flagged by the operator/team** (finding #18) — any new experiment reusing B3/B4 raters' own diagnostics to both write a card's framing *and* judge whether Jev "got it right" needs to route around that, exactly as B4 already does by reserving up to 40 operator-only-labelled posts as the independent check.
- **Cost and latency are settled as non-issues at this scale:** Jev cost $0.024–$0.10 total across all repo experiments so far, comfortably inside the $1 ceiling (`research/Jev cookbooks.md` §Costs) — no need to budget for a "is Jev affordable" question in a card-voting experiment design.
