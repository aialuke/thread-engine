# Reader: LLM choice bias, option order, prompt format, framing, sycophancy

Lens: published research on LLM choice bias, option order, prompt-format sensitivity, framing and sycophancy, read against Jev's five questions. Every paper below was opened. Abstracts were pulled verbatim from the arXiv API, and body quotes come from `pdftotext` of the arXiv PDF. One exception: the PNAS paper's abstract came through Crossref, because the publisher and PMC pages were behind a captcha.

Caveat on transfer: Jev scores named options and returns a probability map. Nearly all of this literature studies generative LLMs that pick a letter or write text. Mechanisms tied to option-ID tokens (A/B/C) probably don't transfer. Position, content, framing and sycophancy effects plausibly do, because they act on how the model reads the context and not on which token it emits.

Q-key: Q1 framing, evidence, risk note and stated lean · Q2 option order and names · Q3 the abstain option · Q4 repeat-call stability · Q5 structured versus plain option descriptions.

## 2. Contradicts our plan (or likely assumptions in it)

1. **Repeat-call agreement is not reliability.** Shi et al. find judges with repetition stability above 0.95 that are still systematically position-biased. If the plan treats "the same answer on 10 calls" as evidence the vote is sound, that's wrong. Q2 (order/name permutation) and Q4 are separate tests, and both are needed. (https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07791)
2. **Jev's `confidence` may not signal fragility.** Normalised entropy didn't predict perturbation sensitivity in 7 of 9 models (Tjuatja). Authority framing *raised* confidence in wrong answers (2601.13433). Warrant is "distinct from verbalized confidence" (2609.04127). A confidence threshold for routing to `ask_luke` needs its own validation: correlate confidence with flip rate.
3. **"Ignore the lean" or a debiasing instruction won't neutralise it.** Anchoring mitigations (CoT, ignore-anchor, reflection) were "not sufficient" (2412.06593, 2505.15392). PriDe's explicit "consider them fairly" instruction left RStd at 6.1 against a default of 5.5. The neutral arm must *remove* the field.
4. **Adding, dropping or splitting `ask_luke` is not neutral to the other options.** Decoy and attraction effects (2409.15299, 2308.00225) and the AOI results (2409.18857) show that an extra option reshapes the distribution over the real ones. Any Q3 comparison needs a metric on the real options' relative probabilities, not only on `ask_luke` mass.
5. **Scoring named options doesn't buy order-robustness for free.** Removing IDs cuts token bias, but position bias "varies with models and tasks" (PriDe). Its direction flips with the quality of the whole option set (Fragile Preferences). Scoring in isolation gave no accuracy gain (2608.11947). Randomise and average over orders anyway.
6. **Structured-versus-plain results from LLMs don't carry over.** Format performance "only weakly correlates between models" (Sclar). Mixing structured and plain options inside one card risks a style bias larger than position bias (2604.23178).
7. **Tracking the evidence is not by itself a good sign.** Judges that follow the prompt-favoured option look accurate exactly when the writer is right (2604.16790). The six-vote observation can't tell good reading from echoing until there are cards where the lean and the evidence disagree.

## 3. Searched, found nothing

- **Q1:** No study of a stated lean or recommendation inside a *structured JSON state* for a non-generative scorer. The nearest are sycophancy papers (a user opinion in chat) and PR-metadata framing (2603.18740). Nothing measured a lean against evidence that disagrees with it for a probability-returning classifier.
- **Q2:** No paper on position or name bias for an API that returns a probability map over *named* options without generating text. Nearest: classifier word-bias (2309.04992), PMI-DC (2104.08315), and name bias in decision prompts (2506.14092). The "Look at the Text" line (2404.08382, 2402.14499) says first-token probabilities and text answers disagree in instruction-tuned models ("mismatch rates over 60%"). Whether Jev's probabilities match what it would "say" is unknowable from outside, and not testable here.
- **Q3:** Within this lens, nothing compares *splitting* an abstain option (for example "need more evidence" versus "options don't fit") with a single one. AOI tested "I don't know" versus "None of the above" versus "I know the answer" wording only.
- **Q4:** No repeat-call study of a classification or scoring endpoint as opposed to a text generator. Everything found (2408.04667, 2502.20747, 2601.19934, 2506.09501) studies generated output. For a single forward pass the expected effect is probability jitter; that is my inference and hasn't been measured.
- **Q5:** No direct test of JSON-structured option descriptions against plain strings *as the option set* of a choice question. Nearest: whole-prompt format (2411.10541, 2310.11324), label descriptions (2305.02239, 2501.12332), and style bias (2604.23178).

## 4. Hand-offs

- **Abstention / calibration reader:** Cherry-pick Override (https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.07834). Judges under-use non-directional verdicts; confidence thresholds leave a residual; a fresh-context check recognises the conflict. This argues for a separate "does any option fit?" call. AOI "I don't know" option (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18857): abstain as a mass sink with the argmax taken over the real options. FairEval Human-in-the-Loop Calibration (https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926): "balanced position diversity entropy" routes the hardest ~20% to a human, "with only a 20% human annotation cost". That is a concrete rule for when to ask Luke: route when votes disagree across orders, not when confidence is low. Epistemic Warrant tiers (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04127).
- **Human survey / decision-methods reader:** Tjuatja's opinion-float and odd/even manipulations (https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.04076). The attraction/decoy effect in LLMs (https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15299). Omission bias and "no" bias (PNAS, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2412015122). The quality-dependent primacy/recency shift and the "robust / fragile / indifferent" preference framework (https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14092), which extends rational-choice theory.
- **TypeSafe docs reader:** check whether Jev's API has an order-averaging option, a way to score one option at a time, or a stated serving-determinism guarantee. Also check whether `confidence` is entropy-based. That decides whether the Tjuatja caveat applies as-is.
