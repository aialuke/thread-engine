# Reader: abstention, none-of-the-above, selective prediction, calibration

Lens: should Jev's `ask_luke` escape option be split (`none_fit` / `too_close`), dropped (use confidence for "too close"), or replaced by a separate Noul "does any listed option fit?". Every paper below was opened; quotes are copied from the PDF/HTML text. All LLM findings are about generative LLMs reading letter options, so they are hypotheses for Jev, which is a scoring model returning a normalised `probabilities` map plus a concentration-based `confidence`.

Key framing, from the reject-option literature: "none fit" and "too close" are two different rejection types that the field has named, measured, and built separate detectors for for decades. **Ambiguity rejection** ("too close") is read off the predictor's own class probabilities. **Novelty rejection** ("none fit") is usually a separate rejector, because normalised class probabilities cannot express "none of these".

## 2. Contradicts our plan

- **"Drop `ask_luke` and rely on confidence" works only for "too close".** F10 finds confidence doesn't fall when the right answer is missing, and F8/F13/F14 agree. So dropping the escape without adding a separate check leaves "none fit", the case that actually happened (the operator later chose off-list), undetected. F19/F20 (vision classifiers) disagree, so the removed-answer test in the plan is the tie-breaker.
- **Splitting into two escape options inside the Choice is the riskiest arm.** F9: the mere presence of an extra option inflates its selection, and a random word does it as well as "Unknown". Two escape options add two such magnets and also split mass between themselves (F7's "probability dispersion"), so neither may clear threshold. F16: escape-option wording strongly moves results.
- **An in-Choice NOTA isn't useless.** F7 finds that adding NOTA to the select step raised Calibration-AUC from 53.17 to 72.59, but it was used as a *penalty score on the winner*, never as a winning option. If the plan compares "keep an escape option" against "separate check", include that variant ("read the escape option's mass, never let it win"), or the keep-arm is a strawman.
- **Conformal prediction sets aren't feasible here.** KnowNo used 400 labelled calibration examples and Szabadváry et al. 500. With 6 votes, use set-size and credibility/confidence as a *descriptive vocabulary* only. Make no coverage claims.
- **Low confidence ≠ needs Luke.** F21: two valid options lower confidence while being right. Whichever design wins, the "too close" outcome should say "both are acceptable", not be treated like "none fit".
- **The plan presumes Jev over-abstains because of genuine uncertainty.** F9 says some share may be artifact. Without a placebo-option arm, the experiment can't tell those apart.

## 3. Searched, found nothing

- **Q1 (framing):** within this lens I found no study that varies the *question stem framing* while holding options fixed and measures the abstain rate on a scoring (non-generative) model. The closest are F25 (system prompt) and F16 (instruction vs option). Hand-off below.
- **Q2 (order/names):** position effects on the escape option are small in the papers found: F9 ≤4.2 pp, F15 about 2 pp. Name effects: F9 (random word = "Unknown") and F12 ("none of the others" prior). I found no study of *semantic* escape names like "ask the human" vs "none of the above".
- **Q3:** I found no paper that directly compares "split abstain into two options" against a single abstain option in LLMs. The split exists only as two *detectors* (F1–F5), never as two *options*. Nor did I find a published head-to-head of Choice+separate-binary-check vs Choice-with-NOTA on a non-generative scoring model. F7 is the nearest, on generative LLM log-probs.
- **Q4 (repeatability):** only F9 (the abstention artifact persists across 3 draws: 66.1% vs 8.2% chance) and F22/F23 (sampling agreement as a signal). Nothing on deterministic scoring APIs.
- **Q5 (structured option descriptions):** nothing in the abstention/calibration literature on structured vs free-text option descriptions changing the escape rate. F13 (least-incorrect selection) is indirect.
- **Confidence summary vs top probability for LLM option-scoring:** no LLM-specific head-to-head of concentration (entropy/Gini) vs max-prob vs top-two margin for ambiguity detection. F17 (image classifiers: Softmax Response > entropy) is the only direct comparison. Ruixi Lin's Gini-index paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.15654) is about class-accuracy imbalance, not confidence, so it doesn't apply.

## 4. Hand-offs

- **LLM choice-bias/framing reader:** F9's position result (≤4.2 pp) and random-word equivalence; F15's shuffled-NOTA drop (64.67→62.62%); Ren et al. 2312.09300's "shuffle and average" debiasing of position; Hanna & Feng, "Accuracy and Order Sensitivity Diverge Under Label-Free Strategies" (2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11947, which says removing positional influence did not reliably improve accuracy; Balepur et al., "Which of These Best Describes Multiple Choice Evaluation with LLMs?" (2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14127, a critique of the MCQ format.
- **Human survey/decision-methods reader:** Tam et al. 2503.01550 cite the education literature on "None of the above" (student scores 0.614→0.418 when NA is correct). Góral et al. 2409.00113 has a human study (>80% missed trick items). Survey research on "don't know" and filter questions (the no-opinion option) is the human analogue of F9.
- **TypeSafe docs reader:** four facts decide transfer. (a) Is a Choice call deterministic? This decides whether F22 applies. (b) Does Noul return an absolute probability independent of other options? This decides whether it can serve as a credibility score (F4). (c) How is `confidence` computed: entropy, Gini, or top-p? This decides F17's comparison. (d) Can per-option Nouls be batched cheaply? That would allow a "does option X fit?" check for each option (a full credibility vector) instead of one "any fit?" Noul.

## Implied experiment arms (for the synthesiser)

1. **A: current.** Choice with `ask_luke`.
2. **B: split.** Choice with `none_fit` + `too_close`.
3. **C: replace (recommended).** Choice over real options only, plus a separate Noul "does any listed option fit this card?". The "too close" flag comes from Choice confidence or top-two margin, with a threshold set from a cost ratio (F18). Evaluate the Noul first (F3).
4. **C′: read-not-win.** Choice with `none_fit` present, but its probability mass is read as a signal and the winner is the argmax over real options (F7, F6).
5. **Placebo control.** Choice with a nonsense-named extra option (F9).

Test cards with known answers: (i) one clearly right option; (ii) two near-equivalent options; (iii) the operator's eventual off-list answer removed / the right option deleted (F10-style). Metrics per arm: pick accuracy on (i), too-close detection on (ii), none-fit hit rate on (iii), false-escape rate on (i) (F15's metrics), and position-shuffle stability.
