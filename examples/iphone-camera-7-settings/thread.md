---

Most iPhones get set up on day one and the Camera app is never touched again.

A wedding guest was about to spend $1,200 on a “real camera” because every reception photo came out flat, crooked, and weirdly wide. The photographer at the next table borrowed the phone for four minutes.

- Didn’t install an app.
- Didn’t edit anything.
- Didn’t switch to Portrait mode.

He changed 7 settings. Then he took the same photo from the same seat.

“Your camera was never the problem. Apple shipped a serious camera with the training wheels still on — grid off, style set to the safest flat look, resolution wound down, and every choice you make gets wiped the second you close the app. Nobody in the setup screens is going to tell you the fix is already in Settings.”

🧵 Here are the 7 settings that fixed it:

---

Setting 1: Grid and Level
The iPhone camera defaults to a blank viewfinder. No guides. So almost every photo lands with the subject dead-center and the horizon a few degrees off.

That is the single fastest way to make a good scene look like a tourist snap.

Go to Settings → Camera.
Turn on Grid.
Turn on Level.

Grid is a 3×3 overlay. Put the face, the plate, or the skyline on a line or an intersection instead of in the middle.

Level is the small bar that turns yellow when the phone is straight. Use it for tables, buildings, water, group shots.

This one change is most of the “how does their phone look better than mine” gap. It is composition, not hardware.

---

Setting 2: AE/AF Lock

Tap the screen once and the phone keeps guessing. Move a step, and the face goes soft or the window blows out.

Tap and hold the thing you actually care about until a yellow banner says AE/AF LOCK.

Focus and exposure freeze. Then slide a finger up or down to brighten or darken without losing the lock.

Same room. Same second. One tap-and-hold stops the phone from renegotiating the photo every time someone walks behind you.

If you only remember one in-camera habit from this thread, make it this one.

---

Setting 3: Photographic Styles

This is not a filter. A filter sits on top of a finished image. A Photographic Style changes how the camera processes colour and tone while the photo is being taken.

Apple ships it on Standard: safe, flat, inoffensive. Fine for everyone. Great for no one.

Open Camera. Swipe up on the controls (or tap the Styles icon). Try Rich Contrast for most daylight shots.

On newer iPhones you also get Tone and Colour sliders. Nudge Tone down a little if skin looks plastic.

Settings → Camera → Photographic Styles is the other door in.

Pick one style and leave it. Consistency beats swapping looks every photo.

---

Setting 4: Resolution

On a lot of recent iPhones the sensor can capture 24MP or 48MP. The camera still defaults lower to save storage.

That is the right default for WhatsApp. It is the wrong default if you crop, print, or zoom a reception photo later.

Go to Settings → Camera → Formats.

If you see Photo Mode, set 24MP for daily use.
If you have a Pro and you actually crop, turn on ProRAW & Resolution Control, then tap 48MP in the Camera app when you want the full sensor.

Do not leave 48MP ProRAW on all day. The files are huge. Use it for the shots you care about.

If Formats has no 24MP or 48MP toggle, your model does not have it. Skip this card. Grid, lock, and Styles still apply.

---

Setting 5: Default focal length

On Pro models the main camera opens at 1x — usually a 24mm-equivalent. It fits the whole table. It also stretches faces and dumps clutter into the edges.

Go to Settings → Camera → Main Camera (some models say Fusion Camera).

Turn on the extra focal lengths and set the default to 28mm or 35mm.

35mm is closer to how a scene looks to your eye. Faces calm down. Backgrounds tighten. You stop including the exit sign behind the couple.

This is a crop of the main sensor, not a different lens. You lose a little field of view. You gain a photo that looks chosen instead of scooped.

No Main Camera menu? Skip it. Use 1x and take one step forward instead of using 0.5x.

---

Setting 6: Volume button as the shutter

Tapping the on-screen shutter is how most blur happens. Your finger moves the phone at the exact moment it needs to be still.

The Volume Up button is a shutter. Press once for a photo. Hold for burst.

Hold the phone like a camera, not like a phone. Index finger on the button. Elbows in.

Burst is how you get one frame where nobody is blinking.

Settings → Camera also has “Use Volume Up for Burst.” Turn that on if you want the hold-to-burst behaviour explicit.

---

Setting 7: Preserve Settings
This is the one that makes the first six actually stick.
By default, close Camera and Apple puts you back to the safe factory setup. Style, exposure, resolution, mode — gone.
Go to Settings → Camera → Preserve Settings.

Turn on:
✓ Grid + Level
✓ AE/AF Lock
✓ Photographic Style
✓ Resolution
✓ 28/35mm default
✓ Volume shutter
✓ Preserve Settings

Now the camera opens the way you left it, like a real camera.

Without this toggle you will do the work once, take three good photos, and be back on training wheels by the speeches.

The full pass.

Grid + Level. AE/AF Lock. Photographic Style. Resolution. 28/35mm default. Volume shutter. Preserve Settings.

Same phone. Same lens. Same wedding. Different photos.

Four minutes. No app. No $1,200 body.

Apple set the Camera app up so anyone can point and shoot on day one. That setup is still running years later unless you change it.
