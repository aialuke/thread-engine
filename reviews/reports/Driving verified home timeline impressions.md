# Aim every original at Premium builders

A Verified Home Timeline impression counts only when one thing happens: a unique checkmarked viewer scrolls past at least half of an original post on the Home feed, and the post is not a reply, not boosted and not fraudulent. X's open-source ranking code, at its 24 Sep 2026 commit, contains **no Premium or verified term for the author or the viewer**. Who the content reaches therefore sets the verified share of an account's impressions; the algorithm plays no favourites. For @exitzerocode, the **337 qualified impressions are about what its 26 verified followers alone could produce** from 11 posts. Only about **14.5% of its 2,329 original and quote impressions qualified**. Reaching 500,000 in 90 days takes about **5,556 a day, 61 times the current pace**. Even if verified followers grew steadily to 500 and each saw two posts a day, they would supply less than a tenth of the total, so **at least 90% has to come from strangers on For You**. The **500-verified-follower goal is realistic**: it needs about 37 a week, against an estimated 26 a week gross in the first week. The **500K goal depends on breakout posts**, so run it as a rolling target. A straight-line climb from 91 to about 11,000 a day crosses 500K within the window, and crossing it later still gets the account in. The legitimate levers, in order of effect:

- point every original at the Premium-dense AI-builder crowd that already makes up 72% of the followers
- put the whole payoff in a standalone root post, ideally native video of the pipeline with the operator's voice-over
- get early likes from a real network of builder mutuals
- make posts that people send to a colleague
- use specific, one-off replies to grow verified followers

What to stop matters just as much: the paid Boost, which cannot count; templated replies and "Drop a hi" posts, which invite spam labels; and politics and device posts that reach viewers who neither pay nor follow.

## Four filters turn a view into a qualified impression

X defines the unit in one sentence: "Qualified impressions are unique impressions from Premium users (subscribers to X Premium Basic, Premium, Premium+, or Premium Business) on the Home Timeline feed, where at least 50% of the post is visible" ([X Help Center, Wayback 16 Sep 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916150107/https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards)). The entry gate uses slightly different words: **"at least 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users in the last 90 days (impressions on replies are excluded)"** plus **"at least 500 verified followers"**, an active Premium, Premium+ or Premium Business subscription, and active original posting ([same page](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916150107/https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards)). The exclusions stack up:

- repeat views by the same account of the same post
- "paid, promoted, or artificially generated" impressions
- fraudulent impressions
- replies
- anything X judges unoriginal, which "won't generate qualified impressions"
- posts that carry a helpful Community Note

The legal terms add that **"any impressions as a result of your content being boosted are excluded"** from payment ([Original Content Rewards Terms §5](https://legal.x.com/en/original-content-rewards-terms.html)).

The one-sentence definition leaves gaps. X never says "For You" or "Following". Both tabs sit under Home, so on the plain wording both count, but X has not confirmed it. Profile and pinned-post views, search, notifications and the conversation (post-detail) view are not "the Home Timeline feed", so on the same wording they probably do not count. The thresholds also name different viewer groups. Payouts count Premium users including Basic, which has no checkmark, while the 500K gate counts "verified users" ([About X Premium, Wayback 16 Sep 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916150103/https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-premium)). Basic viewers therefore probably help pay but not eligibility. There is a live example of the same ambiguity: the coordinator's API read on 24 Sep found 27 checkmarked followers (26 blue, 1 gold business), but X's screen shows 26. That fits either a timing gap or X counting only blue checks, and the evidence cannot tell which. X's 26 is the working number.

Threads are the trap that matters most for this repo. The second and later posts of a thread are replies to the author's own root. On the plain reading, **only the root earns qualified impressions**, and the ranking code agrees: For You keeps one post per conversation, and replies are never indexed for out-of-network retrieval ([dedup_conversation_filter.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/filters/dedup_conversation_filter.rs#L14-L49); [phoenixRankAllCandidateProcessor.strato L440-458](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/phoenix-rankall-strato/columns/phoenix_rank_all/phoenixRankAllCandidateProcessor.strato#L440-L458)). Quote posts are standalone posts, so they should count when they add real commentary. X has not said so for this program. Some things are unpublished:

- when the Creator Studio progress bar refreshes or how far it lags
- the date counting started
- whether "last 90 days" is a rolling window (the plain reading is that it is)

The progress bar is therefore the only authoritative gauge, and no repo file records it over time.

| Where a Premium viewer meets the post | Counts toward 500K? | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| For You or Following, root original, at least 50% on screen | Yes | Official definition; the tab split is unconfirmed |
| Quote post with its own commentary | Probably | Standalone post; X silent for this program |
| Thread cards 2 and later, and every reply | No | "Impressions on replies are excluded" |
| Pinned post seen on the profile, search, notifications, conversation view | Probably not | Not the Home Timeline feed |
| Boosted or promoted delivery | No | Help page exclusion; Terms §5 |
| Same viewer seeing the same post again | No | Uniqueness rule |

## X's ranking has no Premium dial, so audience sets the share

The open-source code shows exactly how an original from a 36-follower account reaches a Home Timeline. Every value below is a default that, per the code's own header, mirrors live feature switches "last sync 2026-09-23" and can be overridden per user or experiment ([param.rs L1](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/params/param.rs#L1)).

**Followers get the post without any engagement.** The in-network store, Thunder, pulls the recent originals of everyone a viewer follows: up to 1,200 results, with at most 50 originals per author ([thunder_source.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/sources/thunder_source.rs#L25-L104); [thunder/config.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/thunder/config.rs#L5-L8)). The Following tab is a plain reverse-chronological search with no scorer ([reverse_chron_posts_pipeline.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/candidate_pipeline/reverse_chron_posts_pipeline.rs#L148-L191)).

**Strangers need a first like.** The main out-of-network retrieval corpus is the `1fav_1day` index. An eligible original enters it on its first like, arriving after a two-minute stream delay, and is re-indexed at 2, 4, 8, 16 and 32 likes, but only within 48 hours ([favoriteEventProcessor.strato](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/phoenix-rankall-strato/columns/favoriteEventProcessor.strato#L14-L176); [retrieval_dataset.py](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/phoenix/xrex/data/retrieval_dataset.py#L247-L277)). A no-like "tail" index for authors under 1,000 followers exists, but nothing in Home references it, so it is not something to plan around ([sid_tail_processor.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/phoenix-rankall/src/processor/sid_tail_processor.rs#L72-L92)).

**Small authors get a modest cold-start lift.** The lift raises one original per feed request to the 16th slot. The post must come from an author with 1,000 or fewer followers, be under 48 hours old, have fewer than 1,000 home views, and already rank in the top 85% of that viewer's candidates ([author_cold_start.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/scorers/author_cold_start.rs#L171-L313)).

**The post then competes on predicted actions, at a discount.** The ranker multiplies each predicted probability by a weight, applies a **0.75 out-of-network discount**, and scales an author's second and third post in the same pool by 0.625 and 0.4375. A diversity pass (DPP) keeps a top set from the best 150 candidates and **sets every other score to zero** ([vm-ranker/params.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/vm-ranker/params.rs#L139-L192); [dpp_model.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/vm-ranker/scoring/dpp_model.rs#L196-L206)). Every For You candidate older than 48 hours is dropped ([age_filter.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/filters/age_filter.rs#L16-L31)). The default weights show which actions matter:

| Predicted action | Default weight |
|---|---:|
| Share via copy link | 20 |
| Reply (on an original from a mutual follow) | 5 + 15 = 20 |
| Reply, quote, share via DM | 5 each |
| Follow the author | 4 |
| Share, repost | 2, 1 |
| Like, click, open link | 0.5, 0.4, 0.2 |
| Profile click, video quality view | 0 |
| Block, not interested, mute, report | −31.2, −43.2, −58.8, −234 |

Sources: [home-mixer param.rs L295-407](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/params/param.rs#L295-L407) and [weights.rs L78-94](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/xai-value-model/weights.rs#L78-L94). The mutual-follow reply boost was launched at 20 on 13 July 2026 and cut to 15 on 24 July ([BIDIRECTIONAL_BOOST_CHANGE.md](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/docs/BIDIRECTIONAL_BOOST_CHANGE.md#L5-L11)). Bookmarks, which the account's settings threads collected, do not appear in the weight list at all. The code also notes that engagement affects ranking only when it happens **"on a post served in Home Timeline. Directly navigating to a post (i.e., coordinating via groupchat) has no ranking impact"** ([param.rs L266-293](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/params/param.rs#L266-L293)).

Premium is absent from all of this. The value model's inputs, the ranker request and the default model features carry no verified or subscription field. The viewer's subscription level reaches only stats and ads logging ([inputs.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/xai-value-model/inputs.rs#L3-L16); [vm_ranker_request.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/scorers/vm_ranker_request.rs#L47-L63)). Verified status appears only indirectly, in four places:

- A credibility PageRank seeds half its trust mass from Premium accounts ([UserCredV2App.scala](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/user-cred-v2/UserCredV2App.scala#L79-L193)).
- An embedding prompt writes "Verified: Blue" into a post's text representation ([renderer_v82.py](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/grox/flows/mm_emb/renderer_v82.py#L70-L265)).
- Verified-only reply settings limit who can reply.
- Some spam-rule exemptions apply, but they cover grey government checks and high-PageRank accounts, **not blue**.

The widely repeated "Premium 4× in-network, 2× out-of-network" boost is attributed to the 2023 code release; this research did not find it there, and the current code has no equivalent. The @monetization_x coaching account does advertise a Premium reply boost in reply sections ([@monetization_x](https://x.com/monetization_x/status/1921532465304514972)), but reply ranking is outside this repo and reply impressions don't count anyway.

The consequence is the central finding of this report. **No ranking mechanism steers a post toward Premium viewers.** The verified share of a post's Home impressions is simply the Premium share of whoever the post reaches. That makes topic and audience selection the only lever on the share, and ranking signals the only lever on the volume.

## The 337 is follower traffic, and the maths demands strangers

The account's entire 90-day window is **3.7 days of activity**. Its first post went out on 20 Sep 2026, and its lifetime tweet count equals the 157 items read since then, so nothing ages out of the window until about 19 Dec ([API backfill](../../ledger/raw/api/backfill-20260924T0120.json)). In that time, 11 originals and quotes earned **2,329 organic impressions**. The "Drop a hi" networking post earned 856, 24 likes, 20 profile visits and **all 13 follows that came from originals**. The iPhone comparison earned 509 impressions but, like the three settings threads, produced **zero root follows and zero root visits** from 1,082 combined root impressions ([activity ledger](../../ledger/activity/2026-09.json)). If each of the 26 verified followers saw each of the 11 posts once on Home, that alone would be about **286 impressions**, and the follower count was lower earlier in the week. The 337 is therefore mostly follower traffic plus a thin verified slice from strangers. The 14.5% overall qualifying rate most likely overstates the Premium share of the stranger audience, and the follower base, at 72% verified, is Premium-dense in a way the Apple and general-tech viewers were not.

Replies were about 81% of the account's reach: **14,220 of 17,482 organic impressions, from 115 replies** ([ledger summary](../../ledger/SUMMARY.md)). That reach counts for nothing toward 500K, and it converted poorly, at 0.3 follows per 1,000 impressions against 6.3 for originals. By topic, networking replies to builders earned **23.7 profile visits per 1,000 impressions**, and Australian politics and news replies earned 2.2. The eight biggest replies, on politics and AI-industry threads, brought 12 visits and no follows ([September audit](../../reviews/audit-2026-09.md); [activity ledger](../../ledger/activity/2026-09.json)). The account has already found, somewhat by accident, where its verified followers come from: builders who pay for X.

| Target | Current pace | Required pace | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qualified impressions | ≈91 a day (337 in 3.7 days) | ≈5,556 a day; ≈5,790 a day from now to 19 Dec | **61×** |
| Verified followers | est. ≈26 a week gross in the networking week; ≈8 a week without that post (19 gross follows at a 72% verified share) | ≈37 a week (474 in 90 days) | 1.4× to 4.6× |

The follower target is within reach of steady builder networking. The impression target is not within reach of incremental gains, and the arithmetic below shows why stranger reach dominates. Suppose verified followers climb in a straight line from 26 to 500 over the 86 days to 19 Dec, and the account posts two originals a day. Even if every verified follower saw both posts every day on Home, followers would supply only about **45,000 qualified impressions, 9% of the target**. At a more realistic half-seen rate they would supply about 22,600. The rest must come from out-of-network For You:

| Premium share of stranger viewers (assumption) | Stranger Home impressions needed | Per post, at 2 originals a day | Against the account's best original (856) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 14.5% (today's overall rate, an upper bound) | ≈3.1M–3.3M | ≈18,000–19,000 | ≈22× |
| 40% (a hypothetical builder-dense audience) | ≈1.1M–1.2M | ≈6,600–6,900 | ≈8× |

Computed from the figures above; the 40% row is an assumption, not a measurement. Two readings follow. First, an even spread is implausible for a 36-follower account; the realistic route is a handful of breakout originals. At a 14.5% share, three posts that each reach about a million Home viewers would cover most of the gap. Second, because the window rolls, what matters is the rate reached, not a single deadline. **A straight-line climb from 91 to about 11,000 qualified impressions a day** adds up to 500,000 over 90 days. If the climb is slower, the trailing sum crosses later, and the program admits on the trailing 90 days at the time of application. It also does not eject an account that later dips below the threshold ([Terms §5](https://legal.x.com/en/original-content-rewards-terms.html)). Industry estimates of the verified share run from 3% to 20% of total impressions. They come from an AI-written X trend summary and from tool vendors with no stated method ([OpenTweet](https://opentweet.io/tools/x-earnings-calculator)), which is too weak to plan on. The account's own progress bar, read weekly, is the only number worth tracking.

## Five legitimate levers, ranked by how much they move

**1. Point every original at the Premium-dense builder audience.** This is the only lever on the share, so it comes first. The account's chosen direction is an Australian builder making an AI idea-to-video pipeline and showing the workflow, the proof and the lessons ([audience promise](../../reference/audience.md)). That is also the audience that has already proven Premium-heavy: 27 of the first 36 followers describe themselves as AI builders, developers or founders. Device tips and iPhone comparisons reach large, mostly non-Premium consumer crowds who don't follow, and politics reaches people who argue but don't convert. For this metric, **build logs, tool verdicts and single tips drawn from the pipeline beat device threads**, however well the device threads perform on raw impressions. X's originality test points the same way: content "you have personally created" that "reflects your own voice, perspective, or expertise" qualifies, and aggregated summaries don't ([X Help Center](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916150107/https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards)). Nikita Bier's dismissal of "the worst Linkedin/AI/threadboy spam" in September 2025 marks the niche's specific failure mode ([@nikitabier, search-indexed](https://x.com/nikitabier/status/1965800163269919203)). First-hand proof is the way around it.

**2. Put the payoff in a standalone root, and make it video with the operator's voice.** Because only the root counts and For You shows one post per conversation, **a multi-card thread's qualified value is its root's reach**. Later cards can earn follows (two of the account's follows landed on mid-thread cards) but never qualified impressions. Native video longer than 10 seconds gets extra like-triggered retrieval indexes on top of the standard ones, which gives a strong clip more routes to strangers inside the same 48-hour life. The default weight on video watch quality is zero, so the gain comes from being retrieved, not from watch time ([eventProcessing.strato](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/phoenix-rankall-strato/lib/eventProcessing.strato#L23-L403); [candidates_util.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/home-mixer/util/candidates_util.rs#L4-L33)). X staff have pushed the same format: Bier called talking videos an "easy way to get millions of impressions with basically no followers" ([PiunikaWeb, 13 Apr 2026](https://piunikaweb.com/2026/04/13/x-twitter-original-talking-videos-nikita-bier/)) and recommended "recording original videos with your own voice over" ([NBC News, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/social-media/x-slashes-aggregator-payouts-boost-original-creators-rcna324820)). That is staff opinion without data, and Buffer's engagement-rate data ranks video below text (2.96% against 3.56%). Engagement rate is a different measure from Premium reach, though ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/data-best-content-format-social-media/)). An account whose product is a video pipeline has an unusually natural version of this format: a screen recording of the pipeline running, narrated by the builder. That makes it the highest-value experiment in the format trial.

**3. Get early likes from a real network of builder mutuals.** The first like opens the stranger pool, and re-indexing at 2, 4, 8 and 16 likes rewards early momentum. A viewer's own follows rank at 1.0× against strangers' 0.75×. A mutual's predicted reply on an original is weighted 20 instead of 5, and X said in July 2026 that it wants mutuals to surface more so that interest clusters can form ([TechCrunch, 13 Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/13/x-just-tweaked-its-algorithm-to-make-it-more-friendly-less-battleground/)). The legitimate version is a few dozen genuine reciprocal relationships with AI builders who see the post in their own feed within its first hour. The illegitimate version is an engagement pod that sends links around in a group chat: by X's own code comment it has "no ranking impact", and it runs straight into the program's ban on manipulating "recommendation algorithms". A side benefit: if the verified share of followers stays near 72%, reaching 500 verified means only about 690 total followers, which keeps the account under the 1,000-follower cold-start cap for the whole run.

**4. Build posts that people send to a colleague.** The heaviest positive weights are predicted copy-link shares (20), DM shares (5), replies and quotes (5 each) and follows (4). A like is worth 0.5 and a profile click nothing. Posts get shared when they contain something reusable: a working prompt chain, a measured before-and-after, a tool verdict with its test. A post that asks one specific question an expert wants to answer earns replies without ever telling anyone to reply. Links are not penalised in the ranker. Bier said in July 2026 that the link penalty was removed "over a year ago" ([Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/tech/x-product-head-nikita-bier-confirms-link-penalty-removed-over-a-year-ago-tells-mark-zuckerberg-he-can-post-them-directly)), and the code has no hard link term. Reply-bait, politics and off-niche takes risk the negative weights, which are the largest in the table.

**5. Use replies to recruit verified followers, not to chase impressions.** Replies add nothing to 500K, but they are how Premium builders discover the account, as the 23.7-visits-per-1,000 networking replies showed. The replies that work are multi-line, specific and early in builder conversations, and every one is different. Graham Mann's anecdote of "12K impressions from one reply. 7 profile visits" makes the same point about reach against conversion ([grahammann.net, Feb 2026](https://grahammann.net/blog/how-to-grow-on-x-twitter-2026); the author sells growth tools).

Timing and cadence finish the plan:

- **Cadence: about two strong originals a day, spaced apart.** Each post lives 48 hours, and the author-diversity decay means a third or fourth post in the same feed request competes with the first at 0.44× and below. The 3–10-posts-a-day advice comes from tool vendors.
- **Timing: test two US windows.** No source measures where Premium users live. The account's replies posted 09:00–21:00 US Eastern averaged about 151 impressions against 26 outside that window, though topic is confounded with time. Buffer's timing study measures engagement in the poster's local time, not the audience's ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-twitter-x/)). For a Brisbane poster, the usable slots are about 10–11 pm (US East morning) and 5–7 am (US afternoon), Tuesday night to Friday morning. Both shift an hour later when US daylight saving ends on Sunday 1 Nov 2026.
- **A deferred format test.** Long-form Articles are a second-tier experiment. X's $1M Article prize in January 2026 was "judged primarily on Verified Home Timeline impressions", which shows X measures Articles on this metric ([Social Media Today, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/x-formerly-twitter-offers-million-dollar-prize-best-article-long-form-post/809930/)). No one has reported how Article impressions register on the progress bar.

## Boosts, copypasta and bait can zero the count

Several habits already visible in the account's first week work against the goal.

**The Boost.** It bought about 2,006 impressions on the tool-swap post, for about 4 profile visits and no extra likes ([activity ledger](../../ledger/activity/2026-09.json)). Those impressions are excluded twice over, as "paid, promoted" and as "boosted". The legal terms also name "promoted post" among the ways of artificially inflating views that can bring **forfeiture and termination** ([Terms §4–5](https://legal.x.com/en/original-content-rewards-terms.html)). While the account chases eligibility, boosting has no upside and carries a clause risk.

**Duplicate replies.** The account sent the same reply text 12 times in three minutes on 22 Sep, posted a bare "👋" six times, and made 68 replies in one 24-hour stretch ([September audit](../../reviews/audit-2026-09.md)). Duplicate replies trip the `COPYPASTA_SPAM` rule, and **blue verification is not an exemption**; only high-PageRank accounts, a skip list and grey government checks are ([BBQDuplicateTextRepliesProd.bot](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/botmaker-rules/scarecrow/bot/BBQDuplicateTextRepliesProd.bot#L28-L33)). The reply-spam scorer sees the replier's 24-hour reply count and whether the text was pasted ([thread.py](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/grox/core/lm/thread.py#L44-L63)).

**The pinned post.** Pinning a post whose link is rated low quality can apply a `SPAM_HIGH_RECALL` user label, and that label **drops every one of the author's posts from Home for everyone** ([PinnedLowQualityOrBadUrl.bot](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/botmaker-rules/scarecrow/bot/PinnedLowQualityOrBadUrl.bot#L26-L41); [author_rules.rs](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/visibility-filtering/rules/author_rules.rs#L101-L108)). The pin should be a link-free builder post, and the account's Under the Hood page (x.com/i/under_the_hood) is the first-party check for any such label ([README](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm/blob/44d37ebf87f2185b949cd37b710d410c2a77d21f/README.md#L444-L448)).

**Engagement solicitation.** Continuing members must not solicit engagement, "repeatedly instructing users to engage with posts, such as asking to like, reply, bookmark, follow, or repost" ([X Help Center](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916150107/https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards)). "Drop a hi" was the account's single best follower driver, and it is also the closest thing it has done to that rule. The voice guide already bans it as a farm tell. It should not be repeated; builder questions that invite a substantive answer are the substitute.

**Content-level rules.** Each of these makes a post ineligible or puts the account at risk:

- **Community Notes.** A helpful Community Note makes a post ineligible, which vindicates the repo's fail-closed fact-checks.
- **Monetisation talk.** Posts "exclusively focused on monetization coaching ... or maximizing payouts" are ineligible, so the account should not post about its own 500K chase.
- **Politics.** The Creator Monetization Standards list "exploitation of controversial political or social issues" as restricted sensitive content ([Creator Monetization Standards, Wayback 24 Aug 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260824022134/https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/content-monetization-standards)). That is one more reason to retire the politics quotes.
- **Automated creation.** This is the biggest unresolved risk for an AI-video account. A post is ineligible if it "Was created or posted using automated means", and X does not define the phrase. Manual posting clears "posted". "Created" is the open question for pipeline output and agent-drafted cards. The defensible position is to make the operator's test, verdict, voice-over and lesson the primary value of every post, so that the generated clip is evidence rather than the product.

**Housekeeping before applying.** The same standards require an account at least three months old (this one was created on 28 Nov 2025), a complete profile with a header image, a verified email and two-factor authentication. Meeting every threshold still "does not guarantee admission": a rejection allows one appeal and a reapplication after 42 days ([X Help Center](https://web.archive.org/web/20260916150107/https://help.x.com/en/using-x/original-content-rewards)).

The evidence has limits. The code values are defaults that live experiments can override. X publishes no refresh cadence for the progress bar and no dataset of verified share by format. The repo's Grok search returned invented posts during this research, so the creator progress reports it surfaced are not used here.

## Conclusion

The Original Content Rewards gate turns X growth into a question of audience composition rather than raw reach. The algorithm offers no Premium lever, so the only way to raise the verified share is to earn the attention of people who pay for X. For this account those people are AI builders, and it found them in its first week through networking rather than through its posts. Raw impressions from politics replies and device threads therefore mislead on this metric. The loop's success measure should be the weekly progress-bar reading, and the verified-follower count alongside it, not views. X reportedly added a Verified filter to account analytics in June 2026, though that report is an unconfirmed AI trend summary ([X trend summary](https://x.com/i/trending/2064093715371631083)). If the filter exists, it is the closest thing to a per-period verified-share gauge.

The two goals run on different engines and should be sequenced that way. Verified followers are the leading indicator and the source of early likes: they come from replies and relationships, which count for nothing toward 500K but decide whether each original gets its first likes and mutual-weighted replies within minutes. Qualified impressions are the lagging, breakout-driven outcome of originals that strangers share, so the realistic plan is 500 verified followers inside 90 days, with the 500K crossing likely falling after 19 Dec unless a pipeline video breaks out. Because the window rolls and admission does not expire on a dip, a late crossing costs nothing except time. The one risk that could void the whole effort is not reach but definition: until X says what "created using automated means" covers, an AI-pipeline account keeps its eligibility by making the human judgement, not the generated output, the thing each post delivers.
